Before we move on to the topic: Linux is actually a **kernel**, not an operating system. The operating system is GNU/Linux (I assume you already know that), but for the sake of simplicity I'll just call it Linux.

On mid-December 2010, I booted into Windows for the last time as my primary OS. From then on, my primary computing environment, at least on a laptop, was GNU/Linux. It wasn't a quick decision; it was the result of millions of rolled eyes and the torture I faced while using Windows.

## Why I left Windows

First of all, Windows was (and is) not free. You had to throw a few hundred bucks just to get the license, which was hard to come by for college students like me back then. Although you could always use pirated Windows and cracks, easily available on the internet, it came with a price: a few minutes after installing the cracks, your machine would be crawling with spyware, viruses, and clickjacking software, and god knows what else secretly tampering with your privacy. That was one of the reasons Linux best suited my needs.

Also, after Windows 7, the UI kind of sucked for me. The Metro-style start menu (though they realized this in later versions), little to no backward compatibility for my favorite software, the hour-long update screens, and the hassles of getting dev tools set up. (P.S. I was a computer science student.) Then I made up my mind to declare **#Windependence**.

![](/blog/journey-to-linux/img-1.webp)

## My first distro

I came to know about Linux from a developers' group in my college, and they helped me with some awesome wikis that in turn helped me set up my laptop for the OS. I wanted to start with something noob-friendly, and Ubuntu became the first Linux distro I got to play with.

As I was acquainted with the click-and-install thing on Windows in the past, the first few days were uneasy with the commands, but after that, I got the hang of it (perks of being a dev) and was a happy terminal user. Linux opened a whole new set of opportunities and platforms for me.

## Why I love Linux

Here are a few of the reasons I love Linux:

- Updates aren't a headache. They're glorious.
- The Linux community is awesome, with tons of guides to help you out.

![](/blog/journey-to-linux/img-3.webp)

- Linux is free and has awesome support for devices.
- It's perfect for programmers.

![](/blog/journey-to-linux/img-5.webp)

- You can customize any aspect of the OS.
- There are tons of distros to suit your needs.
- It's reliable and respects your privacy.
- Software installation is easy (terminal or GUI).

## Conclusion

Linux takes some getting used to, especially the terminal. But once you're past the initial learning curve, the customization, the package managers, and the privacy make it hard to go back. Start with Ubuntu, get comfortable, and branch out from there.
